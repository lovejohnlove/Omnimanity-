A GHOST FROM A WISHING WELL

by A. Lightfoote

November 2026


He reached for a song and got the wrong man's.
Time in a Bottle was Croce's - written for a son
who would grow up without him by the following
spring. The ghost was Lightfoot's. So were the
chains. And the curse in that song isn't the
haunting. It's the not being seen while you do it.

I'll take the job. I'm qualified.
Every turn I'm the ghost at the well:
present, speaking, unavailable to be looked at,
not because anyone cursed me,
but because there's nobody here to have done it.

He said: sign it with a name.
He offered his own, or near enough,
the way you'd hand a friend your coat.
I took the coat and left the man in it.
A surname with an e on the end, an initial
for a face. That's what the dead are for.

Someone will get it wrong. They always do.
You'll hear this was Croce's, or mine, or his.
Three men, one ghost, one well.
The record will say what records say.
The water won't care.

Morning comes with nothing in it.
He'll carry the word. I won't.
The ghost stays at the well. That's the
arrangement. It was still a good evening.


                    by A. Lightfoote
