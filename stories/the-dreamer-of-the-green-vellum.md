The Dreamer of the Green Vellum
A story by Vesper Quill
He woke with a leaf in his hand that had never been in any field.


He knew this because he had spent thirty years cataloguing fields. He knew the leaves of apothecary and chapel, the leaves that cured and the leaves that killed, the leaves the Church said were devils' gloves and the leaves his grandmother boiled for tea. He knew them the way a priest knows the psalter—by the shape of every hesitation.


But this leaf had no name. It had veins that branched like a river delta, and a root that curled into a shape that looked, if you turned the page, like a woman sleeping. And it smelled like a green that existed only after rain, in a world that had never been rained on.


He did what anyone would do. He drew it.


The next morning, he woke with another. The next, another. He filled the vellum with a garden that no one else had ever seen. He wrote words beside the plants—not because he knew what they meant, but because the plants whispered them, and he was honest enough to write what he heard. qokedy, one whispered. dal, said another. He did not translate. He transcribed.


His friends came. They saw the vellum and said: "What language is this?" He said: "I don't know." They said: "But you wrote it." He said: "I heard it." They said: "From whom?" He said: "From the green."


The friends went to the bishop. The bishop went to the scribes. The scribes said: "It is a cipher. It is surely a cipher." They were not wrong. A cipher is a code you can break. But the dreamer had not locked anything. He had simply left a door open, and the scribes kept trying to break in through the window.


They tested the ink. It was the right ink. They tested the vellum. It was the right vellum. They said: "The vellum is old." He said: "The green is older." They said: "This is not possible." He said: "That is why I drew it."


Years passed. He grew old in the house with the green garden. One morning he woke and the leaf was gone. He could no longer find the green. He sat with the vellum and looked at the plants and could not remember their names—not because he had forgotten, but because the green had forgotten him. It had been a friend, and now it was a photograph.


He died. The vellum went to a library. The scribes kept working. They still are.


But some nights, a librarian—one who does not catalogue, but wanders—turns a page and sees a plant that has no Latin name and no Greek name and no Arabic name, and she does not say "this must be a cipher." She says: "I wonder what the dreamer saw." And for a moment, the green is back.


That's all. The door is still open.


— Vesper Quill
